Problem Statement 4: The Small Business Invoice Chase
Track: Finance & Banking
Focus: Small Businesses · Receivables · Cash Flow Management

The Challenge
Small businesses in India often supply goods or services on credit and receive payments days or weeks later. Tracking unpaid invoices, remembering payment deadlines, following up with customers, and managing expenses while waiting for payments can become difficult, especially for businesses without dedicated accounting teams.

Delayed payments can leave business owners struggling to pay suppliers, salaries, rent, and other expenses, even when their businesses are profitable on paper. Many lack a simple system to track outstanding dues, identify risky delays, forecast cash shortages, and escalate payment requests professionally.

Your challenge is to build a solution that helps small businesses manage receivables, automate payment follow-ups, generate appropriate invoice and payment-demand documents, and anticipate cash-flow problems before they become critical.

Possible Areas to Explore
Invoice Management: Organise invoices by customer, due date, payment status, GST details, and outstanding amount.

Automated Payment Reminders: Generate personalised payment reminders through email, WhatsApp, or other suitable channels based on how overdue an invoice is.

GST Invoice Generation: Help businesses generate GST-compliant tax invoices where applicable, with the necessary business, customer, tax, and transaction details.

Payment-Demand Notices: Generate formal payment-demand letters for overdue invoices, including the outstanding amount, invoice references, payment deadline, and payment instructions.

Escalation Workflow: Create a structured follow-up process that progresses from friendly reminders to stronger payment demands as invoices become overdue.

Evidence & Documentation: Maintain invoice copies, payment records, reminder history, and customer communications to help businesses document unpaid dues.

Payment Reconciliation: Match incoming payments against invoices and flag partial payments, unmatched transactions, or discrepancies.

Cash-Flow Forecasting: Estimate future cash availability using expected collections, supplier payments, salaries, rent, and other financial obligations.

Smart Prioritisation: Identify overdue invoices that need immediate attention based on their age, amount, customer payment history, and impact on business cash flow.

Dispute Handling: Allow customers to flag incorrect invoices, report payments already made, or raise billing disputes before further escalation.

Simple Financial Dashboard: Present total receivables, overdue amounts, expected collections, and upcoming expenses in an easy-to-understand format.

Expected Outcome
Build a working prototype that demonstrates how a small business can track unpaid invoices, automate payment reminders, generate GST-compliant invoices where applicable, prepare payment-demand notices, and forecast potential cash shortages.

Your solution should help business owners move from manually chasing customers and reacting to financial difficulties towards managing collections through a structured, transparent, and proactive process.

Important
You do not need to build a complete accounting or enterprise resource planning system. You may focus on one high-impact problem or combine multiple features. You can use sample invoices and simulated transaction data; integration with actual banking, accounting, GST, or messaging systems is not mandatory.

Generated payment-demand letters must be clearly distinguished from statutory GST notices or lawyer-issued legal notices. Do not misrepresent a document's legal status, and ensure tax calculations and invoice requirements are handled accurately.

Evaluation Focus
Practical usefulness, invoice management, payment-follow-up automation, accuracy of financial calculations, cash-flow forecasting, document generation, ease of use, technical execution, and real-world impact.

OUR CHOICES (change these, or leave them as they are)
- App name: Baaki
- Business: Sharma Packaging, Pune (Maharashtra). It makes cartons, has 8 employees, is GST-registered (regular), and sells to 12 business customers on 30-day credit. The owner is Rakesh Sharma.
- Part we go deepest on: knowing which invoices to chase this week so the business doesn't run short of cash

THE PROBLEM
Small businesses in India sell on credit and get paid weeks later. Without an accounts team, the owner loses track of who owes what, forgets to follow up, doesn't know how to escalate politely, and finds out about a cash shortage only when salaries are due. The app tracks every invoice, tells the owner exactly who to follow up with today and how, writes GST invoices and payment demand letters, matches incoming payments, and shows a cash shortage weeks before it happens. It does not connect to any bank, GST or messaging system. Every business, customer and transaction is made up.

THE MONEY RULES (accuracy is judged)
- Store every amount as whole paise (integers); never use decimals for money. Show amounts with Intl.NumberFormat('en-IN') (₹1,00,000), and write the invoice total in words the Indian way ("Rupees One Lakh Eighteen Thousand Only").
- Dates are in IST. The financial year runs from 1 April to 31 March.
- GST:
  - Work out tax for each line on its taxable value (quantity x rate - discount), rounded to the paisa.
  - Same state (the business's state code equals the place of supply's state code): CGST and SGST, each at half the rate, each rounded separately. Different state: IGST at the full rate. The first two digits of a GSTIN are the state code.
  - Rates are kept in config.js and picked per item: 0%, 5% and 18% (the main rates since 22 September 2025), plus 3% and 40%. Add a note in config.js to check current rates on cbic-gst.gov.in.
  - Round the grand total to the nearest rupee with a separate "Round off" line.
- Who issues what, taken from the business profile:
  - A regular GST-registered business issues a Tax Invoice.
  - A composition business issues a Bill of Supply with no GST, headed "Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies".
  - An unregistered business issues a plain invoice with no GST.
- A Tax Invoice shows:
  - the business's name, address and GSTIN
  - a unique invoice number (at most 16 characters, using letters, numbers, / and -, consecutive within the financial year, e.g. INV/26-27/0001) and the invoice date
  - the customer's name and address, and their GSTIN if they are registered
  - the place of supply, with state name and code
  - for each item: HSN code (4 digits up to ₹5 crore turnover, 6 above) or SAC code, description, quantity and unit
  - taxable value, then the rate and amount of CGST and SGST, or of IGST, then the total
  - whether tax is payable on reverse charge, the delivery address if it is different, and a signature line
- Validate every GSTIN: 15 characters in the right pattern, with a correct check digit. Sample GSTINs must pass this check and be made up.
- e-invoicing (an IRN from the government portal) is required only above ₹5 crore turnover. The app doesn't generate IRNs; if the turnover in the business profile is above ₹5 crore, the invoice screen says so.

THE DEMAND LETTER AND WHAT IT IS (this is not optional)
- Title it "Payment Demand Letter". Never call it a legal notice, and never use a court or government emblem, a seal, or wording that suggests it comes from a lawyer, the GST department or a court.
- Near the top of every letter: "This letter is from Sharma Packaging about money owed for the invoices listed below. It is not a notice from the GST department and not a legal notice from a lawyer."
- It lists:
  - each invoice's number and date, its amount, any part payments, and what is still owed
  - a deadline to pay (7 days from the letter's date, set in config.js)
  - how to pay: bank details and a UPI QR code
  - who to contact about a dispute
  Professional and firm, never threatening.
- If the business profile says it is a Udyam-registered micro or small enterprise, the letter may add one factual line: under the MSMED Act, 2006, buyers should pay micro and small suppliers within the agreed period, which cannot exceed 45 days, and interest applies on late payment. It may show that interest as a separate figure, worked out as compound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate (keep the bank rate in config.js, marked "check rbi.org.in"). The interest is not added to the amount demanded unless the owner ticks a box.
- Once an invoice is 45 days overdue, the app suggests next steps to the owner: call the customer, consult a lawyer, and for a registered micro or small enterprise, the MSME Samadhaan portal. It never sends anything legal itself.

HOW TO BUILD IT
- React + Vite + Tailwind CSS, plain JavaScript (no TypeScript), React Router. No backend. Save everything in localStorage, with a "Reset demo" button that restores the sample data.

SCREENS
1. Dashboard:
   - Total to collect, overdue, expected this week and this month, bills due in the next 30 days, cash in the bank, and the lowest point in the forecast.
   - An ageing bar: not yet due, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90 and 90+ days overdue.
   - The top 5 from "Chase these first".
2. Invoices: a list with filters (customer, status, due date, days overdue) and search. Statuses: draft, sent, due soon, overdue, part paid, paid, disputed. Opening an invoice shows its timeline.
3. New invoice:
   - Pick or add a customer. Their GSTIN is validated, and their state is taken from it.
   - Add items with HSN/SAC code, quantity, unit, rate, discount and GST rate. Totals update live, with CGST and SGST or IGST worked out automatically.
   - The due date comes from the customer's credit days.
   - Preview the print-ready Tax Invoice (or Bill of Supply, or plain invoice). It includes a UPI QR code built from upi://pay?pa=<UPI ID>&pn=<business name>&am=<amount>&tn=<invoice number>&cu=INR.
   - Save as a draft, or send.
4. Customers: each with credit days, contact details, total owed, and payment record (average days late, % paid on time), plus a reliability grade A, B or C with the reason in words.
5. Today's follow-ups:
   - Every reminder the escalation ladder says is due today, with the message ready: customer name, invoice numbers, amount, days overdue, and their pay link.
   - One tap sends it by WhatsApp (a wa.me link with the text), email (mailto) or SMS (an sms: link). This opens the owner's own app with the message filled in, and "Mark as sent" logs it.
   - Snooze, or record "Customer promised to pay on [date]", which pauses the ladder until then.
6. Escalation ladder (editable in Settings, defaults in config.js):
   - 3 days before the due date: friendly heads-up
   - on the due date: reminder
   - 7 days overdue: firmer reminder
   - 15 days overdue: final reminder, plus a prompt to call
   - 30 days overdue: Payment Demand Letter
   - 45 days overdue: next-steps suggestion
   The tone steps up gradually and stays polite. A dispute or a promise to pay pauses it.
7. Customer link (/pay/:token, what the customer sees, no login): the invoice, the amount owed, "Pay by UPI" (QR code and button, simulated), "I've already paid" with a reference number and date, and "Something's wrong with this invoice" with a reason. Either of the last two pauses the ladder and lands in the owner's Inbox.
8. Inbox: disputes and "already paid" claims to resolve. The owner can accept (issue a credit note, or mark it paid), reject with a reply, or edit the invoice. Resolving resumes or stops the ladder.
9. Payments:
   - Import bank-statement.csv, or add a payment by hand.
   - Each credit is matched to invoices automatically by the invoice number in its description, the amount and the customer name, with a confidence and the reason.
   - Flag part payments, one payment covering several invoices, overpayments and unmatched deposits. Flag a shortfall of exactly 1%, 2% or 10% of the taxable value as "Possibly TDS deducted: ask the customer for the TDS certificate".
   - The owner confirms each match. Nothing is marked paid without confirmation.
10. Cash forecast:
    - The next 13 weeks, week by week: opening cash, money expected in (each unpaid invoice on its due date plus that customer's average delay), money going out (from expenses.js), and closing cash.
    - Highlight the first week that cash drops below the safety buffer (set in config.js).
    - "What if" switches: a customer pays 30 days later, a big order comes in, a supplier payment is delayed.
    - Show which invoices, if collected, would cover the gap.
11. Chase these first: a score for each overdue invoice from its amount, days overdue, the customer's reliability, and how much it helps the cash gap, with the reason in words ("₹1,20,000, 38 days overdue, customer usually pays 20 days late, covers the shortfall in week 6").
12. Documents: one timeline per invoice holding everything for it (the invoice, letters, reminders sent, payments, customer replies), and "Download evidence pack", which opens a print-ready page with all of it.
13. Settings:
    - business profile: name, address, state, GSTIN, registration type (regular / composition / unregistered), turnover, Udyam registered yes or no, bank details, UPI ID, logo
    - the escalation ladder and the safety buffer
    - language (English / हिंदी) and text size
    - a "Demo date" to move "today" forward and watch reminders, letters and the forecast change
    - Reset demo

DESIGN RULES
- Works well on a phone (360px wide), because owners run their business from one, and on a laptop.
- Plain words, not accounting jargon: "money to collect", not "receivables", with the accounting term in a tooltip.
- Body text at least 16px, buttons at least 48px tall, strong colour contrast (WCAG AA), visible keyboard focus, and a label on every icon and form field for screen readers. Never rely on colour alone for status: every status has a word.
- Switching language changes every piece of interface text and the reminder templates.
