# Top-Three Comparison (eligible set)

Finalists: **Invoice Chase (82.5)**, **Govt Accountability (76.5)**, **mParivahan (74.0)** (Banking at 74.25 is statistically tied with mParivahan; mParivahan taken forward for its higher innovation ceiling — the comparison's conclusions hold for Banking too, more strongly).

## What an average vs excellent team builds

| | Average team | Excellent team | Our edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invoice Chase | Invoice CRUD + reminder emails + a chart (a worse Vyapar) | Vyapar-clone + WhatsApp bot | Escalation ladder with legal teeth (MSMED §15/§16 letters, statutory-interest math, Samadhaan guidance) + collections-probability forecast + everything genuinely automated via scheduled jobs |

## The trade-off in one paragraph

Accountability has the best story and the highest ceiling; it is also the only finalist where the *likely* outcome for a 2-person team is an 70%-finished prototype of simulated machinery — and the official criteria reward a working, explainable build over an ambitious sketch (the organizer's own guide: "small ideas done completely beat big ideas done halfway"). mParivahan is safe but fights the largest crowd with the least differentiated weapon. Invoice Chase gives up a little story for a lot of certainty: every differentiating feature is deterministic logic a 2-dev team demonstrably ships in 48h, it produces real artifacts on camera, it alone is also a natural Render-track entry, and the one finance-industry judge (Aditya Birla Capital product owner) is the judge most likely to recognize the MSMED escalation gap as real.

## Strongest opposing argument per finalist

- **Invoice Chase:** "B2B receivables is boring; a social-impact jury rewards emotion." Rebuttal: the jury is 3 industry product people + 3 academics scoring 5 criteria that favor execution; and ₹7.34 L-cr is not boring.
- **Accountability:** none needed against ambition — the opposing argument is the calendar.
- **mParivahan:** "It's the headline theme; judges may weight the government track." No evidence of track weighting exists; crowd risk is evidenced by accessibility of the statement.
