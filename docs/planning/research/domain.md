## (a) Does GST apply to MSMED s.16 interest? Yes — as part of the original supply, payable on receipt

**Statute (verbatim, CBIC repository):**

- CGST Act **s.15(2)(d)**: "The value of supply shall include … (d) interest or late fee or penalty for delayed payment of any consideration for any supply". https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter4/section15_v1.00.html
- CGST **s.12(6)** (goods) and **s.13(6)** (services): "The time of supply to the extent it relates to an addition in the value of supply by way of interest, late fee or penalty for delayed payment of any consideration shall be the date on which the supplier receives such addition in value." https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter4/section12_v1.00.html · …/section13_v1.00.html
- CBIC **Circular 178/10/2022-GST, para 9** ("Late payment surcharge or fee"): "…it is an ancillary supply naturally bundled and supplied in conjunction with the principal supply, and therefore should be assessed as the principal supply. Since it is ancillary to and naturally bundled with the principal supply … it should be assessed at the same rate as the principal supply." https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/cir-178-08-2022-cgst.pdf (index: https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/node/4899). Para 7.3 of the same circular: a **cheque-dishonour fine is not taxable** — relevant if the team ever adds a bounce fee.
- CGST **s.34(3)**: where "the taxable value or tax charged in that tax invoice is found to be less than the taxable value or tax payable in respect of such supply, the registered person … shall issue to the recipient one or more debit notes"; **s.34(4)**: declare it "in the return for the month during which such debit note has been issued"; Explanation: "debit note" includes a supplementary invoice. https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter7/section34_v1.00.html
- CGST **Rule 53(1A)** particulars of a debit note: (a) supplier name/address/GSTIN; (b) nature of the document; (c) consecutive serial number ≤16 characters (letters, numerals, "-" and "/"), unique for the FY; (d) date; (e) recipient name/address/GSTIN; (f) unregistered recipient: name, address, delivery address, State name and code; (g) "serial number(s) and date(s) of the corresponding tax invoice(s)"; (h) "value of taxable supply …, rate of tax and the amount of the tax … debited to the recipient"; (i) signature. https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter6/rule53_v1.00.html
- Debit notes to registered buyers are reported in GSTR-1 table 9B (the offline tool's `cdnr` sheet): https://docs.infor.com/locally/2025.x/en-us/lclolh/lcllsppov/trd1603693504642.html ; https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs/te9rel66/Tax_India/gst/exporting_gstr-1.htm

**What this means for FullPay (eight consequences):**

1. MSMED interest is **not a separate service**; it is extra consideration for the original invoice. GST applies at the **original invoice's rate** (18 % for auto parts) and with the original invoice's split (CGST+SGST for Punjab→Punjab, IGST for Punjab→Haryana).
2. The GST **liability arises only when the interest is actually received** (s.12(6)/13(6)). So: no GST liability when the letter is generated or when interest accrues. The interest engine must never book GST on accrual, and the letter must say the GST is "payable when received".
3. On receipt, the supplier **must** issue a **debit note** (s.34(3) "shall"), linked to the original invoice(s), with the interest as taxable value and tax at the invoice rate, and report it in GSTR-1 that month (9B). The buyer can take ITC on it like any invoice (subject to CGST s.16 conditions) — so for a GST-registered buyer the net cost of the interest is the interest alone. That is a non-threatening, buyer-friendly fact.
4. **Composition supplier** (Bill of Supply): cannot collect tax; interest simply enters turnover for the composition levy — show no GST line. **Unregistered supplier**: no GST line.
5. **Mixed-rate invoices**: apportion the interest to rate buckets pro-rata by taxable value (no CBIC guidance found either way; disclose the convention in the footnote).
6. **Interest base**: s.16 charges interest on "that amount" — the amount payable under s.15, i.e. the invoice consideration the buyer owes, which includes GST. I found no MSEFC/court guidance that excludes the GST component (search: https://www.scconline.com/blog/?p=302639 and others address principal/interest separation, not GST). Keep the engine's base = **outstanding invoice total incl. GST** and state it in the conventions footnote.
7. **TDS on the interest**: do **not** assert either way. ITAT Ahmedabad in *ITO v. Parag Mahasukhlal Shah* (30-06-2011) held interest on delayed payment of purchase price is not "interest" u/s 2(28A) → no 194A TDS (https://bcajonline.org/journal/2012-65-dtr-ahd-trib-342-ito-v-parag-mahasukhlal-shah-a-y-2005-06-dated-30-6-2011/); Delhi HC 2014 reasoning runs the other way (https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/case_number_pdf/2014:DHC:4226-DB/SKN28082014ITA1792001.pdf). If a buyer deducts TDS on interest, the existing recon TDS flag catches the shortfall.
8. **e-invoicing**: above ₹5 cr turnover, debit notes to registered buyers also need an IRN (NIC schema `DocDtls.Typ` = DBN: https://einv-apisandbox.nic.in/version1.03/generate-irn.html). Demo stays ≤ ₹5 cr (Rev F #3), so only the existing banner applies.

**Exact presentation — letter (Udyam-gated MSMED paragraph, after the per-period interest table):**

> Interest under section 16 of the MSMED Act, 2006, computed to [letter date]: **₹X**
> GST on this interest @18 % (CGST 9 % + SGST 9 %), payable when the interest is received: **₹Y**
> *(unticked)* Amount requested by [pay-by date]: **₹P** (principal outstanding). The interest above is shown for information and is **not** added to the amount requested.
> *(ticked)* Amount requested by [pay-by date]: principal ₹P + interest ₹X + GST on interest ₹Y = **₹T**.
> Footnote: "GST applies to interest for delayed payment as part of the value of the original supply (CGST Act s.15(2)(d); CBIC Circular 178/10/2022-GST para 9). It becomes payable when the interest is received (s.12(6)/13(6)) and will be invoiced by a debit note under s.34(3), on which a registered buyer may claim input tax credit."

Hindi (print terms from the RBI glossary already in the library): "ब्याज पर GST @18 % (CGST 9 % + SGST 9 %) — ब्याज प्राप्त होने पर देय: ₹Y". The GST line is omitted for composition/unregistered businesses; the IGST variant is used when the original invoice was inter-state.

**Exact presentation — engine.** `computeInterest()` stays tax-agnostic (statutory math only). Add one pure function `gstOnInterest(interest_paise, invoice) → { buckets:[{rate_bps, base_paise, cgst_paise, sgst_paise, igst_paise}], total_paise }` that apportions the interest across the invoice's rate buckets by taxable value and applies the invoice's intra/inter-state split; rounding per bucket to integer paise, total = Σ buckets (same discipline as the segments). Config: `gst.on_interest: true`, `gst.on_interest_basis: "original_invoice_rate"`. Golden tests: ₹10,000 interest on an 18 % Punjab→Punjab invoice → CGST 900 + SGST 900; Punjab→Haryana → IGST 1,800; mixed invoice (taxable ₹40,000 @5 % + ₹60,000 @18 %) with ₹10,000 interest → ₹4,000@5 % = 100+100, ₹6,000@18 % = 540+540, total ₹1,280. Pay page shows the same three lines when the letter's checkbox is ticked. `/verify` payee block unchanged.

**Interest debit note** = convenience #2 below (generated only when an `interest` allocation is confirmed in recon, never on accrual).

## (b) Section 43B(h) is now **section 37(2)(g)** of the Income-tax Act, 2025 — use it on the pay page as information, keep it out of the letter

**Primary text (Gazette, Act 30 of 2025, in force 1 Apr 2026 — PIB: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2248005):** https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf

- **s.37(1)**: "The sums payable, as specified in sub-section (2), which are otherwise allowable as a deduction under this Act, shall be allowed as a deduction while computing the income chargeable under section 26 only in the tax year in which such sums are actually paid irrespective of— (a) any provision to the contrary in this Act; or (b) method of accounting regularly followed; or (c) the tax year in which the liability was incurred."
- **s.37(2)(g)**: "amount payable by the assessee to a micro or small enterprise beyond the time limit specified in section 15 of the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006."
- **s.37(3)**: "In case the amounts specified in sub-section (2), **except the sum referred to in clause (g)** thereof, are paid after the end of the tax year … but on or before the due date of filing of return of income under section 263(1) for such tax year, the deduction towards such sum shall be allowed in such tax year." → MSE dues get **no** return-due-date grace.
- Confirmed by the Finance Ministry's Rajya Sabha reply (Unstarred Q. 242, 21 July 2026, MoS Pankaj Chaudhary): 43B(h) "corresponds to Section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act, 2025"; payment period "shall not exceed 45 days"; no relief announced. Reports: https://taxguru.in/income-tax/finance-ministry-clarifies-section-43bh-45-day-msme-payment-rule.html ; https://a2ztaxcorp.net/rajya-sabha-section-43bh-enforces-45-day-payment-timeline-to-micro-and-small-enterprises/ ; 2024 Lok Sabha answer on the 45-day rule (sansad e-library): https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2982875/1/AU91_b4t7y1.pdf
- Related buyer-side facts (MSMED Act gazette, https://www.dcmsme.gov.in/MSMED2006.pdf): **s.22** — audited buyers must show "the principal amount and the interest due thereon (to be shown separately) remaining unpaid to any supplier" and the interest paid under s.16, in their annual accounts; **s.23** — s.16 interest "shall not, for the purposes of computation of income under the Income-tax Act, 1961, be allowed as deduction"; **s.24** — ss.15–23 override any other law. **MSME Form I**: companies with MSE dues beyond 45 days file half-yearly with the MCA "by 31st October for the period from April to September and by 30th April for the period from October to March" (Specified Companies Order 2019 under s.405 Companies Act; copy: https://cdn.taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Payment-to-micro-and-small-enterprise-suppliers-Order-2019.pdf ; 15 July 2024 amendment adds within/beyond-45-day buckets: https://www.corporateprofessionals.com/articles/the-curious-case-of-new-msme-form-i/).

**Recommendation.** Keep Rev F #14 ("drop 43B(h) from the letter"). In a demand letter a tax-consequence sentence reads as leverage, which the pack bans ("never threatening"); it is also irrelevant to buyers who are individuals, under presumptive taxation, or not claiming the deduction. Put it where it is **useful to the buyer**: the `/pay/:token` page, as a collapsed "Why pay by [date]?" note, shown only when the supplier is a Udyam micro/small enterprise **and** the buyer has a GSTIN (proxy for a business). The wording must branch on whether the s.15 limit has passed, because after the limit the deduction has already moved to the year of actual payment:

> **EN (before the s.15 limit):** "Good to know: [Business] is a Udyam-registered small enterprise. For business buyers, section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (earlier section 43B(h)) allows the deduction for purchases from micro and small enterprises only when payment is actually made within the MSMED Act time limit (at most 45 days from acceptance). Paying by [appointed/agreed date] keeps this expense deductible in the year it was incurred. General information, not tax advice."
> **EN (after the limit):** "Good to know: … Because this invoice is past the MSMED time limit, the expense is deductible in the tax year in which it is actually paid — paying before 31 March keeps it in the current year. Any GST on the invoice remains creditable as usual. General information, not tax advice."
> **HI (before):** "जानकारी के लिए: [व्यवसाय] उद्यम-पंजीकृत लघु उद्यम है। व्यवसायी क्रेताओं के लिए, आयकर अधिनियम, 2025 की धारा 37(2)(g) (पहले धारा 43B(h)) के अनुसार सूक्ष्म और लघु उद्यमों से खरीद की कटौती तभी मिलती है जब भुगतान MSMED अधिनियम की समय-सीमा (स्वीकृति से अधिकतम 45 दिन) के भीतर वास्तव में किया जाए। [तारीख] तक भुगतान करने से यह खर्च इसी कर-वर्ष में कटौती-योग्य रहता है। यह सामान्य जानकारी है, कर-सलाह नहीं।"
> **HI (after):** "… यह बिल MSMED समय-सीमा पार कर चुका है, इसलिए यह खर्च उस कर-वर्ष में कटौती-योग्य होगा जिसमें वास्तव में भुगतान होता है — 31 मार्च से पहले भुगतान करने पर इसी वर्ष में। यह सामान्य जानकारी है, कर-सलाह नहीं।"

Owner-side only (ODR card / "why buyers pay on time" panel, never sent): s.37(2)(g) and the s.37(3) no-grace rule; s.22 disclosure in audited accounts; MSME Form I half-yearly filing for companies; s.23 non-deductibility of the interest they pay. ## (d) Legal landmines in the plan — and the fix for each

1. **The word "demand".** "Demand notice" is a term of art in at least three regimes: IBC s.8 (Form 3 / Form 4 under Rule 5 of the Application to Adjudicating Authority Rules 2016 — quoted in an IBBI-hosted order: https://ibbi.gov.in/uploads/order/074ea07c9f16605f4a1c1692ce9e3b50.pdf ; https://blog.ipleaders.in/demand-notice-operational-creditor/); NI Act s.138 proviso (b) — the written demand within 30 days of the cheque return memo, construed strictly by the Supreme Court (https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2018/27023/27023_2018_Judgement_08-May-2019.pdf); GST recovery — the DRC-07 summary under Rule 142(5) "shall be treated as a notice for recovery" (https://gstzen.in/a/rule-142-central-goods-and-services-tax-rules-2017-cgst-rule-142-replaced-as-per-central-tax-notification-162019.html ; MP HC order https://mphc.gov.in/upload/gwalior/MPHCGWL/2020/WP/16117/WP_16117_2020_Order_19-Nov-2020.pdf). **Fix:** keep the pack's exact title "Payment Demand Letter" and the mandated disclaimer; never "Demand Notice", "Notice of Demand", "Statutory Demand", "Final Demand", "Legal Demand", "notice period"; never IBC vocabulary ("operational debt", "default", "corporate debtor"); never NI-Act vocabulary ("dishonour", "within 15 days of receipt of this notice"). Call the 7-day date a "pay-by date". Hindi: "मांग पत्र" (RBI glossary) is fine; never "नोटिस" / "कानूनी नोटिस". Add a unit test that greps every rendered letter/template for the banned list.
2. **ODR eligibility claims.** Never say "you are eligible" or "ODR-ready" as if the portal has accepted anything; say "prepared to the MSE ODR Scheme Guidelines §12.1.4 (a)–(h) checklist; eligibility is decided by the Council". Gates to show, each with its source: Udyam-registered **micro or small** enterprise (s.2(n): a supplier is "a micro or small enterprise, which has filed a memorandum" under s.8 — medium enterprises get nothing); registration **before the supply** — *Silpi Industries* (SC 2021) says yes, *NBCC v. State of West Bengal* (2025 INSC 54, 10 Jan 2025) doubted it and **referred the question to a three-judge bench**, still pending (https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/nbcc-india-ltd-v-state-of-west-bengal-2025-insc-54-unregistered-msme-dispute-resolution-section-18-msme-act-1564497 ; https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/05/07/to-register-or-not-to-register-msmes-and-arbitration-act/ ; https://www.livelaw.in/amp/articles/supreme-court-conflicting-judgments-msmed-act-section-18-reference-analysis-516732) → wording "generally required; the Supreme Court has referred the question to a larger bench"; **traders (NIC 45–47) excluded** — MoMSME OM 2 July 2021 lets them register "restricted to Priority Sector Lending only" (RBI-hosted copy: https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/39016390/OMTRADER07072021.pdf ; https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/notifications/new-definition-of-micro-small-and-medium-enterprises-addition-of-retail-and-wholesale-trade-12126); **Limitation Act applies** (Silpi) → show "time left to file: 3 years from due date minus elapsed" on the ODR card (15 min; see additions).
3. **Interest claims.** (i) s.16 interest exists only for a micro/small supplier registered at the time of supply; for anyone else the letter must show "contractual interest, if agreed" or nothing — gate on `udyam_type`, not `udyam: true`. (ii) Until the owner ticks the box the interest is "shown for information", never "due and payable". (iii) Disclose the base (outstanding incl. GST), day count, rests, rate switches, dispute spans and appropriation (plan already does; add the GST line from (a)). (iv) **s.2(b)**: "where any objection is made in writing by the buyer regarding acceptance of goods or services within fifteen days from the day of the delivery … the day on which such objection is removed by the supplier" is the day of acceptance — so a dispute raised via `/pay` within 15 days of delivery is arguably a written objection that **moves the appointed day**, not merely a paused rung; add an owner-editable `acceptance_date` override with reason "objection removed on …" and a test. (v) A **TDS shortfall is not an overdue amount** — the deductor has paid it to the government to the supplier's credit; once the owner confirms "TDS", record a non-cash allocation (`kind = 'tds'`) that reduces outstanding, so the ladder stops chasing ₹2,000 and the engine stops accruing interest on it. (vi) Use "interest", never "penalty" or "penal interest" (s.16 is compensatory; "penalty" also invites the Circular 178 tolerance debate). (vii) s.15 caps agreed credit at 45 days ("in no case the period agreed upon … shall exceed forty-five days"); where an invoice carries 60-day terms the reminder due date and the statutory clock diverge — footnote it: "interest runs from the statutory appointed day even where agreed terms are longer". (viii) s.16 applies "notwithstanding anything contained in any agreement" and s.24 overrides other laws — so never offer an "interest waiver clause" toggle; offer only "don't add interest to this letter".
4. **Letter wording.** Banned: "legal notice", "legal action", "default/defaulter", "hereby called upon", "failing which", "constrained to", "without prejudice", "under instructions from", "penal", "dishonoured", "without further notice", any MSEFC/ODR/43B(h) threat (Rev F #14 stands). Keep: the pack's disclaimer verbatim near the top, "please confirm receipt", dispute contact, Udyam line with type and date, acceptance dates. State GST figures from the stored invoice, never recomputed in the letter.
5. **DPDP wording.** "Designed to DPDP principles — duties not yet in force" stands. Three refinements from the Gazette text (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2023/248045.pdf): **s.2(t)** "personal data" is "any data about an individual who is identifiable" — GSTIN, company name and invoice totals are not personal data; the contact person's phone/email are. **s.17(1)(a)**: "The provisions of Chapter II, except sub-sections (1) and (5) of section 8, and those of Chapter III and section 16 shall not apply where— (a) the processing of personal data is necessary for enforcing any legal right or claim" — collecting a lawful debt is exactly that, so when the duties commence (13 May 2027, G.S.R. 843(E): https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/267647.pdf) reminder processing is largely exempt; FullPay still gives notice and honours channel opt-outs voluntarily. Therefore **never use "consent" as the basis** on the pay page or privacy card (a buyer cannot "withdraw consent" to being reminded of a debt); use "we process your contact details to collect a lawful debt; you may choose the channel or opt out of automated reminders". Keep s.8(1)/8(5) (lawful purpose, safeguards) — they are not exempted.
6. **s.37(2)(g) / 43B(h)**: information on the pay page only, branched by date as in (b); never in the letter or WhatsApp templates (Meta rejects "legal action" framing and restricts "debt collection"; the library already covers this).
7. **MSME Form I / s.22 / s.23**: owner-side only.
8. **TDS treatment of interest**: never asserted (see a.7).
9. **Composition / unregistered supplier**: no GST-on-interest line, no debit note; the plan's document-type switch must also gate these.

## Candidate additions

- **GST-on-interest line in the letter, pay page and engine (s.15(2)(d) / s.12(6) / Circular 178 para 9)** (Technical Execution, ~40 min, demo-visible: True) — Add pure `gstOnInterest(interest_paise, invoice)` beside `computeInterest` (buckets by the invoice's rate × taxable value, CGST+SGST or IGST per the original place of supply, integer-paise rounding per bucket, total = Σ). Letter (Udyam-gated paragraph) prints three lines: 'Interest under s.16 … ₹X', 'GST on this interest @18 % (CGST 9 % + SGST 9 %), payable when the interest is received: ₹Y', and the requested total that includes ₹X+₹Y only when the add-interest box is ticked; footnote cites s.15(2)(d), s.12(6)/13(6), Circular 178 para 9 and s.34(3). Omitted for composition/unregistered businesses. Goldens: ₹10,000 interest @18 % same-state → 900+900; other-state → IGST 1,800; mixed 40k@5 %/60k@18 % → ₹1,280. _Why:_ A CA or finance judge checking 'accuracy of financial calculations' will look for exactly this; every free s.16 calculator and the PayUpPal prior art ignore GST on interest. It also proves the 'no LLM in the money path' discipline with one more reproducible test.
- **TDS confirmation becomes a non-cash allocation (`kind = 'tds'`) that closes the shortfall** (Technical Execution, ~30 min, demo-visible: True) — When the owner confirms a recon row flagged 'Possibly TDS', write a `payment_allocations` row of kind `tds` for the shortfall (base = taxable value, rate from config, section label s.393(1) Sl.), reducing `outstanding` so the ladder stops and `computeInterest` excludes it from the base from that date. ## Risks

- **Letter or pay page books GST on interest as already due (or omits it entirely)** (L: medium, I: A CA judge marks the 'accuracy of financial calculations' criterion down; a real owner would under-collect GST (if omitted) or misstate a liability that only arises on receipt (s.12(6)/13(6)).) — Separate statutory interest (s.16) from GST on interest (s.15(2)(d)); print the GST line as 'payable when received'; issue the debit note only on confirmed receipt; footnote the provisions.
- **Statutory-interest paragraph shown for a non-eligible supplier (medium enterprise, trader, or registered after the supply)** (L: medium, I: Misrepresents a legal entitlement in a letter the pack requires to be accurate; undermines the novelty claim if a judge toggles Settings to 'medium'.) — Gate on `udyam_type ∈ {micro, small}`, a 'wholesale/retail trader' flag, and `udyam_registered_on ≤ earliest acceptance date`; otherwise print 'contractual interest, if agreed' or nothing.
- **ODR card over-claims eligibility while the Supreme Court question is open** (L: medium, I: Legal inaccuracy visible on the hero screen; a lawyer judge will know NBCC (2025) referred the registration-timing question to a larger bench.) — Wording 'generally requires Udyam registration before the supply (Silpi Industries, 2021); the Supreme Court has referred the question to a three-judge bench (NBCC v. State of West Bengal, 2025 INSC 54)'; never 'you are eligible'.
- **Chasing and charging interest on a TDS shortfall after the owner confirmed it as TDS** (L: high, I: Demo shows a reminder for ₹2,000 that is legally already paid; interest engine overstates the claim.) — Non-cash `tds` allocation on confirm reduces outstanding and the interest base from that date; dashboard excludes it from cash received.
- **The word 'demand' or lawyer phrasing drifts into templates or Hindi strings** (L: medium, I: Breaks the pack's non-negotiable rule and collides with IBC s.8 / NI Act s.138(b) terms of art; WhatsApp templates with 'legal action' are rejected by Meta.) — Keep the pack's exact title and disclaimer; banned-phrase unit test over rendered letters and templates; Hindi uses 'मांग पत्र', never 'नोटिस'.
- **Pay page or letter cites 43B(h) as leverage or in the wrong tax year** (L: low, I: Reads as a threat (pack rule) and, after the s.15 limit, the 'pay now to keep the deduction this year' claim is false unless paid before 31 March.) — Letter: none (Rev F #14). Pay page: date-branched informational note citing s.37(2)(g) and s.37(3), ending 'not tax advice'.
- **Privacy card frames reminders as consent-based** (L: medium, I: Legally wrong basis (debt recovery is an s.17(1)(a) exempt purpose once in force) and absurd UX (a debtor 'withdrawing consent' to reminders); a privacy-literate judge will notice.) — Use 'notice + channel choice + opt-out of automated reminders', quote s.17(1)(a) and s.2(t) on the card, keep 'designed to DPDP principles'.
- **Asserting TDS treatment of MSMED interest** (L: low, I: Contested law (ITAT Ahmedabad vs Delhi HC reasoning); a wrong assertion on a printed document is a credibility hit.) — Say nothing about TDS on interest; let the recon flag handle any shortfall.
- **GSTR-1 export headers rejected by the offline tool** (L: medium, I: A judge who tries the export gets 'Column Headers Missing/Mismatch'; the convenience backfires.) — Copy headers verbatim from the template bundled with the current offline tool; label the export 'for the GST offline tool template v[x]'.
- **Interest footnote silent on the base (GST-inclusive) and on invoices with agreed terms longer than 45 days** (L: medium, I: A buyer or judge asks 'why interest on the GST?' or 'why day 46 when terms say 60?' and the letter has no answer.) — Footnote: base = outstanding invoice total including GST (the amount payable under s.15); interest runs from the statutory appointed day even where agreed terms exceed 45 days (s.15 proviso).
